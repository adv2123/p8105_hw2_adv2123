The NYC transit dataset contains information on each subway entrance and
exit. After cleaning, it has 1868, rows and 19 columns, of which 15 are
character variables (line, station name, routes 1–11, vending, and
entrance type), 2 are logical variables (entry and ADA compliance), and
2 are numeric variables (station latitude and longitude). To clean the
datam, I imported the file specifying that blank cells be treated as
missing and that routes 8-11 be read as character variables. These
columns contain numbered trans and would otherwise be read as numeric,
even though the values are rout name and not true numbers. I then
cleaned the variables names, retained only the variables of interest,
and converted the entry variable from character to logical. Given that
route spread across 11 columns, we can conclude that this data is not
tidy. To be tidy, there should be a single column for the variable
route.

The combined dataset contains 1188 observations and 15 variables. The
data were collected from 2014 to 2025, and each observation represents a
dumpster filled by one of three trash wheels: Mr. Trash Wheel (707
dumpsters), Professor Trash Wheel (132 dumpsters), and Gwynnda (349
dumpsters). Some key variables include the trash wheel that collected
the dumpster (`trash_wheel`), the dumpster number and the date of
collection, the weight (in tons) and volume (in cubic yards) of trash
collected, as well as the count of specific items, such as plastic
bottles, polystyrene, cigarette butts, plastic bags, sports balls, and
wrappers. Some variables are not recorded for every wheel. For example,
sports balls are only available for Mr. Trash Wheel.

For available data, Professor Trash Wheel collected a total of 282.26
tons of trash. Gwynnda collected 18,120 cigarette butts in June of 2022.
